# Johnny Tremain — the lighting designer's brief (Theatre 2)

For the agent that designs the rig and writes the QLC+ file. The scenes are in [BREAKDOWN.md](BREAKDOWN.md), the ground
plan in [`assets/johnny-tremain/set.json`](../../assets/johnny-tremain/set.json) ([format](set-format.md)).

**Frames.** Stage frame: **+x = stage right** (house left), **z = metres upstage of the proscenium plane** (negative =
apron), y up from the deck. **World = (20.00 + x, 0.80 + y, 2.30 + z).** Hanging positions below are given in WORLD
metres (Theatre 2 is the Midtown scan at x +20), with the stage-frame value where it helps.

**The show's light in one paragraph.** One wooden unit set, upstage; everything else is the company standing where
the walls would be, so **light makes the places**: twenty-six scenes, most of them two to seven places each, with
changes under ten seconds done in light, in view. Johnny talks to the house all night — he needs to be findable
anywhere, instantly, without the scene going dark. The comedy is lit warm and open; the five turns that are not
jokes (the burn in 6, the execution in 20, Otis in 19, Rab in 25, the finale) get stillness and isolation. Period
practicals — a furnace, candles, lanterns, torches, two church lanterns — carry the night scenes. No music but a fife,
bells, a drum and whistling: light cues ride the sound cues (the rooster, the clock striking eleven, the shutters'
bangs, the volley, the dates called like a clock in 21).

## 4. Colour and time of day

A period palette: firelight and candlelight are the warmest things in the show (orange-amber, the furnace a deeper
orange than any candle); daylight is a clean straw-white (Boston harbour, salt air), never theatrical pink; night is
a steel blue, not a saturated one; dawn is grey-lavender warming to straw. The British are scarlet in a world of
brown and grey: do not tint the stage red. The four cups are always the coolest, cleanest white on stage.

## 5. Movers, gobos and haze

- **Water** (4, 13, 14, 18, 24): a slow ripple on the apron and the downstage floor, steel blue by night,
  blue-green by day; it should spill a little onto the front rows ("the harbour is the house"). Two movers on the
  bridge with a water gobo + rotation, or gobo rotators.
- **Fire** (6, 17, 20, 24): flicker on the furnace and the torches' side-light; the crucible's hot snap in 6.
- **Night and moon** (4, 13, 17, 19, 21, 24): a moon-and-cloud breakup from high and behind; the moon is "up" in 21.
- **Leaves** (18, 25): a leaf breakup over the bench / the Green.
- **Jail bars** (11) and **window** shapes (21, 22, 25, 26): gobos.
- **The ride** (20): the runaway is the second act's biggest laugh and must end in total stillness — movers chasing
  ahead of the company-horse, then everything snaps to one still pool at the gate. (Scene 12's lap of the yard and
  the hurdles in 20 are smaller versions; Revere's rides are narrated, not staged.)
- **Lexington** (21 end: one shot, a volley, in the dark before dawn; 25: the Green by afternoon; 26: the sunset
  finale): the volley is sound with the faintest far flash; the Green is leaf-light; the finale is the long light.
- **The steeple** (21): the two lanterns are the cue, not the cut-out: light the lanterns, barely the steeple.
- **The volley and the eight eyes** (20): flash, then isolation.
- **Haze:** a light, even haze all night (the wharf, the furnace's smoke, the harbour) helps the shafts — the
  window light, the doorway in 23, Rab at the back in 26, the moon. Keep it thin in the interior scenes.

### Sightlines the rig must respect

- The front row's eye is at stage (0, 0.32, −4.30) — barely over the deck, 4.3 m from the arch — so it sees **up to
  y = 0.32 + 0.949 (z + 4.3)** through the arch (5.1 m at stage z 0.7, 10.5 m at the back wall), and with the masking
  border at stage z 3.0, **0.32 + 0.559 (z + 4.3)** upstage of it. Every proposed electric (y 7.2 over the deck, stage z 0.6–6.0) is
  above that line; the venue's iron at 5.1–5.4 is below it, so the front rows see it downstage of the border.
- The back row's eye (stage 0, 3.1, −16.3) sees no more than ~4.4–4.9 m up at any depth: the loft's actors (heads at
  ~4.1) and the steeple's lanterns (3.45–3.9) are inside that.
- The widest stalls seats (world x 11.4 / 28.5 at z −4.5…−5.5) look into the wings past the legs.
